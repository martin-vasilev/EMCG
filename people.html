People

Researchers and students

The group brings together students and researchers interested in eye movements, attention, reading and quantitative methods. Our projects also connect to a wider network of collaborators across universities and disciplines.

Group lead

Martin R. Vasilev

Lecturer in Experimental Psychology · University College London

Martin studies how people control their eyes during reading and visual tasks, and what eye movements reveal about attention. His current work focuses on auditory distraction, saccade targeting and regressions during reading, individual differences in attentional control, and methods for scalable webcam eye tracking.

His research combines experimental psychology with quantitative modelling, open-source software development and collaborative projects spanning psycholinguistics, attention and computational methods.

Collaborative research environment

A wider network beyond a single laboratory

Many of our projects are carried out with collaborators at other universities and across disciplinary boundaries. This allows students to combine close day-to-day supervision at UCL with access to complementary expertise in psycholinguistics, oculomotor control, computational modelling, open-source eye tracking and applied research.

For prospective PhD students, this means that a project can be intellectually broader than the immediate local group while still having a clear research home at UCL.

Collaboration is part of the research model

We aim to build projects around the expertise they need, rather than restricting questions to methods available within one lab.
