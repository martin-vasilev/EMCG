Research

How do the eyes reveal cognitive control?

Our work uses eye movements as a fine-grained measure of cognition. We study how visual and auditory information shapes attention, how readers plan and execute saccades, and how computational models can explain the resulting behaviour.

Attention and auditory distraction

We investigate how unexpected, deviant and intelligible sounds interfere with ongoing visual behaviour. A central question is how rapidly auditory novelty changes the timing of eye movements and what this tells us about attentional control.

Eye movements during reading

We study the mechanisms that guide the eyes through text, including return-sweep saccades, parafoveal processing, word-level influences and the coordination of eye movements in multiline reading.

Saccade targeting and oculomotor control

Our experiments ask which visual and linguistic cues readers use when selecting saccade targets, and how oculomotor constraints interact with language processing.

Computational modelling

We use computational and statistical models to move from descriptive eye-movement effects toward mechanistic explanations of attention and reading behaviour.

Remote eye tracking

We are interested in how lower-cost webcam eye tracking can extend eye-movement research beyond the laboratory while preserving sensitivity to established cognitive effects.

Evidence synthesis and open science

Meta-analysis, Bayesian inference, reproducible workflows and openly shared code are integral parts of our research programme.
