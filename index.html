Eye-Movement

We investigate how attention, language and oculomotor control shape eye movements during reading and visual cognition.

Research

What we study

Eye movements during reading

Fixation timing, saccade targeting, multiline reading, return-sweep saccades and lexical influences on visual selection.

Read more

We study how readers decide where and when to move their eyes, and how linguistic information is integrated across successive fixations.

Current themes: return-sweep targeting, parafoveal processing, word-frequency effects, oral reading, and individual differences in reading behaviour.

Attention and auditory distraction

How unexpected sounds, speech and music affect reading and the control of eye movements.

Read more

We examine how task-irrelevant sounds capture attention and alter the timing and planning of eye movements during reading and scene viewing.

Current themes: auditory novelty, intelligible speech, background music, saccadic inhibition, and the time course of attentional capture.

Remote and accessible eye tracking

Reliable eye-movement research using webcams and other lower-cost technologies outside specialist laboratories.

Read more

We test when consumer webcams can provide useful measures of reading and visual behaviour, and how those measures compare with laboratory-grade systems.

Current themes: fixation detection at low sampling rates, remote calibration, data quality, online experimentation, and scalable assessment.

Computational modelling

Models of fixation detection, attentional states and individual differences using statistical and machine-learning approaches.

Read more

We use computational models to describe how eye movements unfold over time and to identify patterns that are difficult to capture with summary measures alone.

Current themes: hidden-state models, sequence learning, fixation classification, predictive modelling, and model validation.

Clinical applications

Using eye movements as objective markers of attentional control, particularly in ADHD and digital assessment.

Read more

We investigate whether eye-movement measures can complement conventional assessments by providing objective information about attention and cognitive control.

Current themes: ADHD, symptom severity, digital phenotyping, remote monitoring, and clinically useful task design.

Open and reproducible research

Evidence synthesis, transparent workflows, shared materials and software for robust eye-movement research.

Read more

We develop resources that make eye-movement research easier to reproduce, evaluate and extend across laboratories.

Current themes: preregistration, open data and code, meta-analysis, reusable software, and transparent reporting.
